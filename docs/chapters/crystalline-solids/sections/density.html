Section 3.5 — Theoretical Density
p. 57

Every crystal has a unit cell with a fixed number of atoms and a fixed volume. Dividing the mass of those atoms by that volume gives the theoretical density $\rho$. Because it depends only on atomic weight and geometry, it can be computed before a sample is ever weighed.

The logic is a two-step ratio. First, find the mass inside one cell: $n$ atoms each weighing $A/N_A$ grams. Then divide by the cell volume $V_C$. The four quantities — $n$, $A$, $V_C$, $N_A$ — completely determine the result.

Cell volume $V_C$ comes from the lattice parameter $a$, which is tied to the atomic radius $R$ by hard-sphere geometry:

  • FCC: $a = 2\sqrt{2}\,R$ → $V_C = 16\sqrt{2}\,R^3$
  • BCC: $a = \dfrac{4R}{\sqrt{3}}$ → $V_C = \dfrac{64R^3}{3\sqrt{3}}$
  • HCP: $V_C = 6\sqrt{3}\,R^2 c$ with $c \approx 1.624 \times 2R$

Why it matters
A single formula links atomic weight and crystal geometry to a macroscopic, measurable property — and any deviation from the theoretical value is a quantitative fingerprint of the defects within.

Density is geometry made measurable.
